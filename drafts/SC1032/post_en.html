Sizing guide

Choose your size by your hip measurement: the elastic takes care of the waist. The pants are drafted for knits, so the hip is close to body size and the stretch gives the comfort. Between two sizes, take the larger one, and go one size up in thick sweatshirt fleece or a knit with less than 20 % stretch. Not sure? Read how to choose your size.

Fabric and notions

Fabric amounts were calculated by laying out the real pattern pieces of every size in each group, single layer, plus about 8 % extra. Sizes 8–10 need only about 1.2 m on 150 cm fabric. Fabrics with a nap or a one-way print may need a little more.

Elastic length

Add 2 cm (3/4″) for the overlap. Wrap the elastic around your waist before joining it and adjust for comfort.

Common fit adjustments

Length. The pants are drafted for a height of 168 cm (5′6″), with a side length of about 92 cm (36 3/8″). Cut the front and back halfway between the crotch and the knee notches and spread or overlap by the same amount, then redraw the side seam and inseam. Small changes can also be made at the hem.

Thick fabric. In sweatshirt fleece thicker than 5 mm, or a knit with little stretch, choose one size up and trim the seam allowances inside the casing to reduce bulk.

Materials

  • Main fabric: 1.2 to 1.9 m (1 3/8 to 2 1/8 yd) of 150 cm wide fabric, depending on your size: a medium-weight knit with good recovery, such as French terry, modal sweat knit or ponte
  • Pocket bags: 0.3 to 0.4 m (3/8 to 1/2 yd) of cotton lawn, lining jersey or a lighter knit
  • Elastic: 3 cm (1 1/8″) wide, length per size in the table above + 2 cm
  • Fusible stay tape 1 cm wide, about 50 cm, for the pocket openings (optional)
  • Polyester thread, a ballpoint or stretch needle and a 4 mm twin stretch needle (optional)

Design details and fit of the barrel pants

The pattern is drafted for medium-weight knit fabrics, so you can sew it on a regular machine with a stretch stitch or on an overlocker. There are no side panels and no zipper: just a front and a back for each leg, pocket bags hidden in the side seams and a casing folded straight from the pants for the elastic. In our photo we made it in a soft, matte rust brushed knit.

  • Pull-on pants with a fold-over elastic waist casing
  • Curved barrel leg, roomy at the knee and tapered at the hem
  • Two hidden in-seam side pockets
  • Front and back crotch seams, no side panels
  • 3 cm (1 1/8″) waistband elastic
  • Simple turned hem, 2 cm (3/4″)

The fit is close at the hips and relaxed through the leg, with the extra width at the knee that gives the barrel shape. The leg opening is about 32 cm around in size 8, so the hem sits neatly on the ankle with sneakers or loafers.

Sewing notes

Fit & style

Close at the hips, curved through the leg, tapered at the ankle. Choose by your hips.

Fabrics

Medium-weight knits with good recovery: French terry, modal sweat knit, ponte. No wovens.

Cutting & sewing

Ballpoint needle, narrow zigzag or overlocker, clips instead of pins. Let the machine feed the knit.

Choosing fabric for barrel pants

A medium-weight French terry or a modal sweat knit with a brushed back is the ideal choice: it has enough body to hold the curved leg, drapes softly and springs back after stretching. Do the stretch test: 10 cm of fabric should stretch to at least 12 cm across the width and recover. Ponte or double knit gives a smarter pair for the office, and sweatshirt fleece makes cosy winter pants if you go one size up.

Avoid wovens, since the pattern has no zipper and relies on the stretch to pull on, and very thin jersey, which will cling instead of holding the barrel shape. Cut the pocket bags from cotton lawn, lining jersey or the main fabric if it is not too thick.

Sewing steps

Seam allowance 1 cm (3/8″) unless stated otherwise. Sew with a narrow zigzag, a stretch stitch or an overlocker, right sides together, and press with an up-and-down movement.

  1. Prepare and mark. Transfer all markings and copy the two pocket circles onto each back side seam. Optional: fuse stay tape along the front side-seam edges to stop the pocket openings stretching.
  2. Pocket bags. Stitch a pocket bag to each front and each back along the side seam, the top of the bag on the casing line. Press the bags outward and understitch the front ones.
  3. Side seams and pockets. Sew the side seam from the top to the upper circle, then stitch the pocket bags together around the curve and continue down the side seam to the hem. Clip the back allowance at the circles and finish the edges.
  4. Inseams. Fold each leg and sew the inseam from the crotch to the hem, matching the knee notches. Turn one leg right side out.
  5. Crotch seam. Slip one leg inside the other and sew the whole crotch seam in one go. Add a second row in the lower curve and trim close to it.
  6. Waist casing. Finish the top edge, turn it under 1 cm, fold the casing on the FOLD line and stitch on the casing line, catching the top of the pocket bags. Leave 5 cm open at centre back.
  7. Elastic. Thread the elastic through the casing, overlap the ends 2 cm and zigzag them together, then close the opening and spread the gathers evenly.
  8. Hem and press. Fold the 2 cm hem up without stretching it and stitch with a twin stretch needle or a coverstitch. Give the pants a final light steam press.

Tips and common mistakes

Wavy seams. Do not pull the fabric while sewing. Let the machine feed it, lengthen the stitch a little and use a walking foot on thick knits.

Stretched pocket openings. Fuse a strip of stay tape along the front edge before you sew, and add a small bar tack at each end of the opening.

Elastic rolling in the casing. Stitch in the ditch through the casing at the side seams and centre back to hold the elastic flat.

Skipped stitches. Change to a new ballpoint or stretch needle: a universal needle often skips on knits.

Care

Machine wash cold or at 30 °C (86 °F), inside out, on a gentle cycle. Dry flat or tumble dry low, and press with steam and a cool iron if needed.

Questions

Is seam allowance included?

Yes: 1 cm (3/8″) on all seams, 1.5 cm (5/8″) on the outer curve of the pocket bags, and a 2 cm (3/4″) hem allowance.

Which paper sizes are included?

The free download comes in A4 and US Letter, as a printable PDF with one file per size, XXS to 4XL (US 00–22).

Which size should I choose?

Choose by your hip measurement; the elastic adjusts the waist. Between two sizes, or in thick fleece, take one size up.

Can I use a woven fabric?

No: the pants have no zipper and need the stretch of a knit to pull on. Use a knit that stretches at least 20 % across the width.

How much fabric do I need?

From 1.2 m (sizes 00–6) to 1.9 m (sizes 8–22) of 150 cm wide fabric, plus 0.3–0.4 m for the pocket bags. See the fabric table above.

Do I need an overlocker?

No. A narrow zigzag or a stretch stitch on a regular machine works for every seam, and a twin needle gives a neat stretchy hem.

Are these barrel pants good for beginners?

Yes. There are only three pieces and no zipper. The side seam with the in-seam pocket is the trickiest step: read it once before you start and watch the video guides included in the tutorial.
