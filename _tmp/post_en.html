Body measurements

Finished garment measurements

Bust, waist and hem width are full circumferences of the closed jacket. Back length is measured at centre back from the neck seam to the hem; sleeve length along the sleeve. Drafted for a height of 168 cm (5′6″).

Fabric and notions

Fabric amounts were calculated by laying out every piece of each size single layer, on grain, plus about 8 % for shrinkage and matching. Allow extra for a nap, pile or a plaid to match. Prewash or steam-shrink the wool and the lining first.

Common fit adjustments

Length. The jacket is drafted for a height of 168 cm (5′6″), with a back length of about 52.5 cm (20 5/8″). Add or remove the same amount at the hemline of every body piece, the front neck facing and the lining pieces, and buy a zipper to match. Change the sleeve length above the vent on all four sleeve pieces.

Thick wool. Size up for a thick coating, trim the interfacing out of the seam allowances on the collar and stand, and use a walking foot.

Design details and fit of the cropped jacket

The Cropped Zip Jacket is a tailored, cropped layer for autumn and spring. The collar is sewn between two stand pieces, so the zipper runs right up into the stand and the inside stays clean. It is fully lined with a bagged lining. In our photo it is made in a rust wool.

  • Cropped length, just above the hip
  • Shirt collar on a separate stand
  • Exposed separating zipper from the hem to the top of the stand
  • Princess seams front and back, with a small shaping dart at center front
  • One-piece center back, no back seam
  • Two vertical single-welt pockets with lined bags
  • Two-piece sleeve with a 10 cm (4″) vent at the wrist
  • Full bagged lining with a center back ease pleat, drafted for 1 cm shoulder pads

The fit is close but comfortable, with about 9 cm of ease at the bust: wear it zipped up or open over a tank, a fine knit or a shirt.

Sewing notes

Fit & style

Fitted and cropped. Choose by your bust, size up for thick wool.

Fabrics

Medium-weight wool, flannel, melton, tweed, twill or corduroy, with a smooth lining.

Cutting & sewing

Interface the shell, baste the welts and zipper first, press with a cloth and ham.

Choosing fabric for a cropped jacket

A medium-weight wool or wool blend with a soft, brushed surface gives the look of our photo: wool flannel, melton, a light coating or felted wool all press well and hold the princess seams. Tweed, bouclé, medium cotton twill or corduroy also work for a more casual jacket.

For the lining, choose acetate, viscose (bemberg) or a polyester twill lining: they slide over your clothes and breathe. Avoid stretch fabrics and very thin fabrics for the shell. Always test the interfacing on a scrap of your wool: no bubbles, no shine, no glue through.

Sewing steps

Seam allowance 1 cm (3/8″) unless stated otherwise. Sew right sides together, backstitch at both ends and press every seam before crossing it with another one.

  1. Fuse and mark. Fuse interfacing to pieces 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 through a pressing cloth, then re-mark the dart, pocket box and notches.
  2. Front darts. Sew the small dart on each center front and press it down over a ham.
  3. Front princess seams. Join each side front to its center front, clip the curves and press the seams open.
  4. Single welt pockets. Make each welt, stitch it and the outer pocket bag on the pocket box, cut the opening, turn through, then add the inner bag and close the bags.
  5. Back princess seams. Join the side backs to the one-piece center back and press open.
  6. Shoulders and sides. Sew the shoulder and side seams, matching notches and hemlines.
  7. Sleeves and vent. Join upper and lower sleeves, fold up the 3 cm hem and the vent, and catch-stitch them by hand.
  8. Set in the sleeves. Ease the caps into the armholes and tack in the shoulder pads.
  9. Collar. Sew the two collar pieces on three sides, trim, turn and press.
  10. Collar stands. Sandwich the collar between stand A and stand B and stitch the top edge.
  11. Stand to the jacket. Sew stand A to the jacket neckline, ends exactly on the front edges.
  12. Zipper. Baste the zipper halves to the fronts, bottom stop on the hemline, teeth up to the top of the stand.
  13. Lining with facings. Sew the facings to the lining, form the back ease pleat and assemble the lining and its sleeves, leaving a turning opening in one sleeve. Sew stand B to the lining neckline.
  14. Join the front edges. Stitch the lining unit to the jacket along the fronts and the stand ends, through the zipper tape.
  15. Hems. Sew the lining to the body hem with a small ease fold, and slipstitch the sleeve linings around the vents.
  16. Turn and finish. Turn the jacket through the sleeve opening, close it, stitch in the ditch of the neck seam and give a final press through a cloth.

Tips and common mistakes

Wavy zipper. Baste it first and close it before stitching: the hems, pockets and neck seam must line up on both sides.

Puckers at the welt corners. Clip right up to the last stitch, but not through it, then stitch the small triangles firmly to the bag.

Bulky collar points. Grade the seam allowances and trim the interfacing out of the allowances before turning.

Shiny wool. Always press through a cloth and let the wool cool flat before moving it.

How to style the cropped jacket

Everyday: zipped open over a white tank with straight blue jeans and a straw tote, like in our photo.

Smarter: over a fine knit dress with ankle boots, or with wide wool trousers and a turtleneck.

Care

Dry clean, or air and brush the wool after wearing. Steam rather than wash and press through a pressing cloth. Hang it on a shaped hanger with the zipper closed.

Questions

Is seam allowance included?

Yes: 1 cm (3/8″) everywhere except the pocket bags (1.5 cm), plus a 4 cm hem on the body and 3 cm on the sleeves. Lining pieces have no hem allowance.

Which paper sizes are included?

The free download comes in A4 and US Letter, as a printable PDF with one file per size, XXS to 4XL (US 00–22).

Which size should I choose?

Choose by your bust measurement and check the finished measurements. Take one size up in thick wool or to wear the jacket over a warm sweater.

Is the jacket lined?

Yes, it is fully lined with a bagged lining and a center back ease pleat, so the inside is as neat as the outside.

What zipper do I need?

One separating (open-ended) metal zipper of 45 cm (18″). Shorten it from the top for the smaller sizes.

How is it different from the other cropped zip jacket?

This one has a shirt collar on a stand, vertical welt pockets and a two-piece sleeve with a vent. Our other version has a simple stand collar.

Is this jacket good for beginners?

It is an intermediate project. The trickiest parts are the welt pockets and the collar, stand and zipper: take your time and baste first.
