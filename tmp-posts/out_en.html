Sizing guide

Choose your size by your bust measurement, then check the hips: the vest is relaxed, with plenty of ease for layering over a shirt or a fine knit. In a thick fabric (bulky bouclé, heavy tweed, boiled wool) or if you will wear it over a warm sweater, go one size up. Not sure? Read how to choose your size.

Body measurements

Finished garment measurements

Waist, hips and hem are full circumferences. Front length is measured from the high shoulder point to the hem; back length at centre back. Drafted for a height of 168 cm (5′6″).

Fabric and notions

Fabric amounts were calculated by laying out every piece of each size on the fabric width, plus about 8 % for straightening and shrinkage. Allow extra for one-way designs, large checks or stripes.

Common fit adjustments

Length. The vest is drafted for a height of 168 cm (5′6″), with a front length of about 74 cm (29″) in size 8. Add or remove the same amount on the Front, Back and Front Neck Facing, about 15 cm (6″) above the hem, and redraw the side seams.

Bulky fabric. In thick bouclé or coating, take one size up, sew with a 2.8–3 mm stitch, grade the seam allowances inside the facings and use clips instead of pins.

Materials

  • Main fabric: 1.1 to 1.8 m (1 1/4 to 2 yd) of 150 cm wide fabric, depending on your size: a medium-weight woven with some body, such as wool bouclé, tweed, wool flannel or linen
  • Fusible interfacing: 0.9 to 1 m (1 to 1 1/8 yd), light to medium weight, for all the facings
  • Buttons: 9 buttons 1 cm (3/8″), 10 for sizes 20–22
  • Matching all-purpose thread, a hand-sewing needle for the hem
  • Overlocker or zigzag stitch to finish the raw edges (optional)

Pattern pieces

  • 1 Front, main fabric: cut 2 (a mirrored pair)
  • 2 Back, main fabric: cut 1 on a single layer
  • 3 Front neck facing, main fabric + interfacing: cut 2 + 2
  • 4 Front armhole facing, main fabric + interfacing: cut 2 + 2
  • 5 Back neck facing, main fabric + interfacing: cut 1 + 1
  • 6 Back armhole facing, main fabric + interfacing: cut 2 + 2

The Back and Back Neck Facing are drawn whole: cut them on a single layer, not on the fold. Transfer the dart legs and points, button marks, CF line and notches.

Design details and fit of the long vest

The Longline Button Vest is a tailored layer you can wear all year. It has a straight, hip-length body, a one-piece back with no centre-back seam, and facings at the neckline, front edges and armholes instead of a lining, which keeps it quick to sew. In our photo it is made in a navy textured wool.

  • Longline, straight silhouette that sits at the hip
  • Round, collarless neckline
  • Centre-front button closure: 9 small buttons (10 in sizes 20–22)
  • Side bust darts and back shoulder darts
  • One-piece back
  • Interfaced facings at the neckline, front edges and armholes
  • Sleeveless, made for layering

The fit is relaxed, with about 15 cm of ease at the waist in size 8, so it slips over a shirt or a rib knit and can be worn buttoned or open. Wear it with a belt for more shape.

Sewing notes

Fit & style

Straight, relaxed and hip-length. Choose by your bust, size up for bulky fabrics.

Fabrics

Medium-weight wovens with body: wool bouclé, tweed, flannel, linen, twill. No stretch.

Cutting & sewing

Interface every facing, staystitch the curves, press each seam with a pressing cloth.

Choosing fabric for a long vest

A textured wool, wool bouclé or tweed gives the cosy autumn look of our photo: it holds the straight shape and hides the facings well. For a lighter vest, choose a medium-weight linen, cotton twill or a fine corduroy; these are washable and good for spring.

Avoid stretch fabrics and very thin or slippery fabrics such as satin or viscose challis: the vest needs body to hang straight, and the buttonholes need a stable fabric. Always test the interfacing and one buttonhole on a scrap first.

Sewing steps

Seam allowance 1 cm (3/8″) unless stated otherwise. Sew right sides together and press every seam before crossing it with another one.

  1. Interface and staystitch. Fuse interfacing to all the facings (3, 4, 5, 6) with a pressing cloth. Staystitch the neckline and armholes of the Fronts and Back, 0.8 cm from the edge.
  2. Darts. Sew the bust darts on the Fronts from the side seam to the point and press them down. Sew the two shoulder darts on the Back and press them toward the centre.
  3. Shoulders. Join the Fronts to the Back at the shoulders, finish the edges and press open.
  4. Facing units. Join the Front Neck Facings to the Back Neck Facing, and each Front Armhole Facing to a Back Armhole Facing, at the shoulders. Finish the outer edges flat.
  5. Prepare the hem. Finish the lower edge and press the 2 cm hem up to mark the fold line, then unfold.
  6. Front and neck facing. Pin the neck facing unit to the vest and stitch in one continuous seam: across the lower end on the hem fold, up the front, around the neck and down the other front. Trim the corners, grade and clip the curve.
  7. Understitch and turn. Understitch the neckline, turn the facing inside and press, rolling the seam slightly to the inside.
  8. Armholes and side seams. Sew the armhole facings with the sides still open, understitch and turn. Then sew each side seam in one go, from the edge of the facing down to the hem.
  9. Hem, buttons and press. Tack the facings inside, hand-sew the hem with a catch stitch, make horizontal buttonholes on the right front, sew the buttons and give the vest a final steam press.

Tips and common mistakes

Facing peeking out. Understitch as far as you can and roll the seam slightly to the inside when you press.

Bulky lower corners. Trim the corners diagonally and grade the seam allowances before turning, then push them out gently with a point turner.

Dimple at the dart point. Do not backstitch at the point: sew the last centimetre on the fold and knot the threads.

Shiny marks on wool. Always press through a pressing cloth with an up-and-down movement.

Care

Follow the care instructions of your fabric. Wool: dry clean or gentle hand wash and dry flat. Linen and cotton: machine wash at 30 °C (86 °F). Press with steam through a pressing cloth.

Questions

Is seam allowance included?

Yes: 1 cm (3/8″) on all edges and a 2 cm (3/4″) hem allowance on the Front and Back.

Which paper sizes are included?

The free download comes in A4 and US Letter, as a printable PDF with one file per size, XXS to 4XL (US 00–22).

Which size should I choose?

Choose by your bust measurement and check the hips. The vest has generous ease; take one size up in bulky fabric or to wear it over a sweater.

Is the vest lined?

No. The neckline, front edges and armholes are finished with interfaced facings, which is quicker than a lining and keeps the vest light.

How much fabric do I need?

From 1.1 m (sizes 00–6) to 1.8 m (sizes 16–22) of 150 cm wide fabric, plus 0.9–1 m of fusible interfacing. See the table above.

How many buttons do I need?

9 buttons of 1 cm (3/8″) for sizes 00–18, and 10 for sizes 20–22.

Is this long vest good for beginners?

Yes, for a confident beginner. The trickiest step is the one continuous seam that attaches the front and neck facing: take it slowly and pivot exactly on the hem fold line.
